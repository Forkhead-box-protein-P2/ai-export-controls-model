Model notes

How the game works

This independent Codex model integrates a finite set of possible paths. Its probabilities and welfare points express the assumptions you choose.

Players, timing and information

In 2026 the US commits to a licensing rule. China evaluates three sourcing strategies over four years, weighing accessible compute, domestic know-how and security against investment and diversion costs. A softmax rule gives higher-scoring strategies more probability. Firms then procure and learn each year through 2050. The adaptive US rule responds to last year’s relative capability. Each side uses its own decision rule. This is a scenario comparison with bounded planning.

Compute, learning and transition

Equipment and memory restrict chip output through the tighter bottleneck. Buyers choose domestic deployment according to availability, quality and mandates. Retired chips leave the stock. Cloud rentals transfer services across borders for one year. Domestic production and actual use add fresh learning experience. Each year, frontier capability changes the chance of a first transformative advance. Exclusivity, initial durability and subsequent decay determine where its probability flows.

Risk, values and what the model leaves open

Conflict starts from an annual baseline. Restrictions raise tension and can strengthen deterrence. Catastrophe risk depends on competitive closeness and transition probability. Each event is charged once at first incidence. The model prices both risks while continuing the technology paths. It omits wartime destruction, irreversible catastrophe feedback and any payoff after the selected horizon.

Annual value combines US and Chinese economic surplus, third-country spillovers, open access, strategic discretion and the two risk costs. The discount rate is 3% from 2026. The lead weight can favor either country. Industrial worlds have fixed weights of 25%, 50% and 25%. All numerical inputs are illustrative.

Evidence, equations and reproduction
