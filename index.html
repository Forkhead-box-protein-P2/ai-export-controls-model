Do AI export controls pay?

Controls on chips, high-bandwidth memory and chipmaking equipment buy the US some extra months of AI capability lead, and the extra lead fades. It is worth something only if transformative AI arrives while the lead still exists, and only to the extent a lead is decisive or makes development safer. Controls also cost lost output, weaker open-model competition and a change in war risk every year they are in force. This page puts all of that into fifteen numbers you can move.

Every default comes from a 190-parameter engine run under one of four worldviews, and each is traced to the mechanism that produces it. Most engine inputs are illustrative rather than measured, so read results as if these numbers, then this answer. The useful output is which numbers flip the answer. The disagreement

One side, fox, holds that "AGI" need not be strategically decisive: improvement and diffusion may continue for decades with no lasting winner. On that view, controls cost open-model competition and trade, give China a captive market to learn on, and may raise conflict risk. The other side, Erich Grunewald, holds that transformative AI is fairly likely to be decisive and that a large US lead buys time to make it safe. His stated forecast is that controls keep a US compute advantage of roughly 10× or more for a decade and 2× or more through the 2040s. The worldview presets below put these positions into numbers. They are illustrative operationalisations, not anyone's elicited priors.

What the lean model leaves out

  • Path dependence. A lead is summarised by its starting size and a half-life. The engine's lead path can rise, plateau or turn negative, and its value is not linear in the lead's length: a decisive advantage saturates, and so does the safety slack.
  • China's strategic response. Mandates, subsidies, retaliation, evasion and Beijing's changing beliefs are folded into the lead numbers and the war term. In the engine's parameter sweep these were second-order, but only under the engine's own assumptions about how China responds.
  • Signalling, tension and interdependence as states. These appear only through the single war-risk change.
  • Industrial detail. HBM, packaging, yields, equipment and power are collapsed into the compute multiple and its half-life. About sixty engine parameters reduce to "how many months of lead do controls buy, and how fast does it fade".
  • Outcome regimes and war types. Distributed or near-simultaneous arrival, durable versus dissipating leads, and denial versus capture wars are averaged into the value scalars.
  • Catastrophe risk that moves with tension. In the engine, a close race is more dangerous when tension is high, and controls raise tension. Easing every control lowers it, so the engine credits Broad relaxation with some safety that the lean safety term, which sees only the lead, misses.

Where the numbers come from

The engine is a year-by-year stochastic game: US policy, China's response, domestic adoption, know-how and output, compute and capability, the arrival, decisiveness and durability of a transition, war and catastrophe hazards, and a weighted payoff vector. For each worldview, it is run on 3,000 paired worlds and reduced to the fifteen numbers below. Explore the full engine, or see the source, configs and tests.

Does the lean formula reproduce the engine?

Net advantage of controls, $tn present value, for the selected worldview. The yearly-cost and war terms are read from the engine for each comparison, so they match by construction. The test is whether the lead and safety terms, with the value of a month of lead and the risk cut per month calibrated on Maintain vs Relax chips only, carry over to the other comparisons and value sets.

Engine checks

A sweep of every ranged engine input found the problems below. Each has been fixed or traced to a sourced assumption.

  • Power is measured in physical terms. China's datacentre power is set against the US's in gigawatts, not as a multiple of its own 2026 need, so less efficient Chinese chips need more of a fixed supply. Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 uses about 2.5 times the power per FLOP of Nvidia's NVL72. Each country builds power toward what its hardware needs, keeping its 2026 spare capacity, no faster than a maximum rate. China starts with about three times the power its chips need, so in the main worldviews it never runs short, as SemiAnalysis argues. Chip efficiency then matters only if that spare capacity is smaller; the stress test where Chinese power binds shows it.
  • US power is a real crux. US build-out is tight from 2027 to about 2031: the hardware that power can run falls to about 70%. While US power binds, chips sold to China cost the US no usable compute, which weakens the case for controls. That is why US power growth is among the largest levers in the decisive worldview. Its range now rests on the LBNL datacentre electricity projections, plus a faster AI share and allied sites.
  • China can lead. How good Chinese labs' own methods are now varies across worlds. China leads at arrival in about 1% of worlds in the continuous worldview, under 1% in the decisive one and 3 to 5% under wide uncertainty, and more often when controls are eased. Terms that need a Chinese lead are live but small, because a Chinese lead needs both better methods and a narrow compute gap.
  • Large levers now carry sources, but remain judgements. The follower floor is anchored to Epoch's smallest observed lag of four months. Chinese research spillover is scaled against the AI Index's count of notable models. The race multiplier rests on game-theoretic work with no empirical size. The openness effect on catastrophe risk sits at zero between two opposing reviews. Treat all four as cruxes.
  • No dead parameters. The first sweep never ran Tighten or Adaptive, so parameters that act only there looked dead. Across all comparisons, only the near-simultaneous window has no effect, by design: it only labels outcomes. A few others matter only in one branch (a capture war, a Chinese lead, scarce Chinese power, a packaging bottleneck), and their definitions say so.

Sweep: every ranged input at its low and high bound, two worldviews, four comparisons (Maintain against Relax chips and Broad relaxation; Tighten and Adaptive against Maintain), two value sets.
