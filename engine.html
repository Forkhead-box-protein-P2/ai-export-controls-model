Do AI export controls pay? A strategic model of the whole game

Chip controls clearly slow China's access to compute in the near term. The harder question is whether the strategic value of the US lead they produce outweighs the direct and dynamic costs of keeping it, across the AI futures we might be in. This model plays the game out year by year. The US sets controls on chips, high-bandwidth memory (HBM), chipmaking equipment (SME) and cloud access. China responds. Industry, compute and capability evolve. A transformative transition may or may not arrive, and it may or may not be decisive. War and AI catastrophe run as hazards throughout. Every policy is evaluated on the same simulated worlds.

Most parameters are illustrative test inputs, not measured estimates. Each is labelled in Assumptions as observed, estimated, elicited or illustrative, with its source. Read results as conditional statements: if the world works like this, then this policy does better. The point is to show which beliefs flip the answer.

The disagreement being modelled

The model grew out of a public exchange about chip controls. One side, fox, holds that "AGI" need not be strategically decisive. Technology could keep improving and diffusing for decades, with no lasting winner. On that view, controls may give China a captive market that speeds its domestic learning. They may also weaken the Chinese open-weight models that compete with closed US providers, and make Taiwan a more important prize. The other side, Erich Grunewald, holds that transformative AI is likely to be very consequential and fairly likely to be decisive. A large US lead buys time to make systems safe. His stated forecast is that the current controls will keep a US compute advantage of roughly 10× or more over the next decade and 2× or more through the 2040s, and that the advantage would be much smaller if chip, HBM and SME controls were eased.

A real mechanism is not yet an equilibrium. The two worldview presets below put these positions into numbers. They are illustrative operationalisations, not anyone's elicited priors. Only the compute-lead figures above are stated forecasts, and in this model they are outputs of the industrial module, never imposed.

Reading the channels

Provocation: controls raise tension, and tension raises the hazard. Strategic denial: if Beijing believes AI is decisive and the US depends on Taiwanese fabs, cutting China off from those chips gives it a reason to deny them to the US. Deterrence: a US capability lead raises China's expected costs. Interdependence: trade ties make confrontation costlier. Signalling enters through China's belief, which moves with how hard the US works to protect its lead.

Reading the safety channels

The hazard is summed over the US and Chinese frontier developers. Each channel is switched off one at a time on the same simulated paths. Chinese developer: the extra risk from the nationality multiplier on Chinese development. Lead slack: the safety a lead buys, counted only to the extent it is spent on safety. Race: extra risk from a close race under tension. Openness: the open-model effect, if any. Exposure: everything else, mainly how close each developer gets to the threshold (controls keep Chinese labs further from it), plus interactions.

Structure

Time runs in annual steps from 2026 to 2050, plus a terminal value covering the years after 2050, so a short horizon does not favour short-run gains. Every policy starts from the same inherited 2026 state: earlier restrictions, sunk investments and Beijing's procurement mandates do not disappear when a policy knob goes to zero. A US policy is a vector \(x_t=(x_{\text{chips}},x_{\text{HBM}},x_{\text{SME}},x_{\text{cloud}},x_{\text{enf}})\in[0,1]^5\), where higher is more restrictive. Smuggling and diversion leak a share of each restriction; enforcement shrinks that share, and China's evasion effort grows it.

  1. The US sets \(x_t\). The adaptive policy reacts to a noisy reading of last year's capability lead.
  2. China responds through an assumed behavioural rule: a procurement mandate, R&D and subsidy effort, retaliation and evasion. These rise with supply insecurity \(h_t\), which jumps when controls tighten and fades slowly when they ease. R&D also rises with Beijing's belief \(b_t\) that AI is decisive.
  3. Domestic industry produces what its bottlenecks allow.
  4. Chinese buyers split demand between domestic chips and permitted imports.
  5. Hardware stocks, power, compute services and capability update.
  6. Hazards of transition, catastrophe and war are drawn.
  7. Payoff flows are recorded and the state advances.

Adoption: is the market captive?

The share of Chinese buyers who would choose domestic hardware is

\[ s_t=\sigma\!\Big(a_0+b_m\,m_t\,(1-\varepsilon\,\text{acc}_t)+e_h\,h_t-d_q\ln\frac{q^{\text{imp}}_t}{q^{\text{dom}}_t}\Big). \]

Here \(m_t\) is the mandate, \(\text{acc}_t\) is effective access to imports, and \(\varepsilon\) (mandate erosion) is how much of the mandate's force is lost when better imports are actually on offer. With \(\varepsilon=0\), Beijing secures the same captive market with or without imports, which is the decisive worldview's assumption. With \(\varepsilon\) near one, availability of US chips undermines the mandate, which is the continuous worldview's. Blocked import demand partly spills over to domestic chips. A chip is counted once, as domestic or imported, and imports become usable at the start of the next year.

Know-how and output are different things

Know-how indices for logic, HBM, equipment and software are measured relative to the moving frontier, from 0 to 1. Each grows with R&D, with learning from new experience, and with spillovers from foreign tools:

\[ K_{t+1}=(1-\delta)K_t+r\,\rho(y_t)\,\tau_t\,(1-K_t)+\lambda\Big[\ln\!\big(1+\tfrac{E_{t+1}}{\bar E}\big)-\ln\!\big(1+\tfrac{E_t}{\bar E}\big)\Big]+\kappa\,(1-x^{\text{eff}}_t)(1-K_t). \]

With no new experience, no R&D and no spillovers, know-how cannot grow, because the same historical deployments are never counted twice. Software learning comes from operating time on domestic chips, weighted by how many leading buyers actually use them. That is the captive-market channel. Output is a separate quantity. It is the minimum of the logic, HBM and packaging bottlenecks in chip equivalents, times final yield, times per-chip performance \(e^{-\gamma(1-K_{\text{logic}})}\). SME controls slow capacity expansion and HBM controls cap the memory supply, so controls can speed know-how while cutting output.

Compute is not capability

Installed hardware is depreciated each year. Power caps how much of it can run, so more electricity cannot help without chips. Power is measured in what a 2026 Western chip could run, and China's is set against the US's in physical terms, so a less efficient Chinese chip needs more of a fixed supply. Each country builds power toward what its hardware needs, keeping its 2026 spare capacity, no faster than a maximum rate. Rented foreign cloud capacity adds services in the year it is rented and never enters China's hardware stock. Capability, in natural-log units above the 2026 US frontier, is

\[ \ln A^{US}_t=\alpha\ln\frac{T^{US}_t}{T^{US}_0}+G^{US}_t,\qquad \ln A^{CN}_t=\max\!\Big(\min\big(\alpha\ln\tfrac{T^{CN}_t}{T^{US}_0}+G^{CN}_t,\;\ln A^{US}_t-\phi\big),\;\alpha\ln\tfrac{T^{CN}_t}{T^{US}_0}+G^{US}_t-\gamma\Big). \]

A 10× training-compute ratio maps to a capability gap of only \(\alpha\ln 10\), measured in years of frontier progress. China's 2026 algorithmic index reproduces the observed lag of about seven months, and diffusion keeps it near that steady state. Borrowed efficiency (imitation, distillation, published research) can keep a follower close to the leader but cannot carry it past. China leads only if its own compute overcomes the intrinsic gap \(\gamma\), which is a world type: it varies across worlds around its median, so in some worlds Chinese labs' own methods are better.

Arrival, decisiveness and durability are separate draws

A transition arrives when frontier capability crosses an uncertain threshold \(\theta\). Its median is solved so that P(transformative AI by 2035 under Maintain) equals the worldview's stated value, and some worlds have no threshold at all. Decisiveness \(d\in[0,1]\) is a world type drawn from a two-regime mixture. The leader's durable capture share and its advantage after arrival are

\[ k=d\,\big(1-e^{-\ell/c}\big)\,(1-\omega O),\qquad a_t=k\cdot 2^{-(t-t^*)/H}. \]

Here \(\ell\) is the lead in years at arrival, \(O\) is open-model competitive pressure, and \(H\) is a persistence half-life correlated with \(d\). A world can be transformed with no durable winner: if open models are close to the frontier, the frontier is distributed.

War and catastrophe are hazards

\[ h^{\text{war}}_t=\sigma\Big(\theta_0+\theta_T(T_t-T_0)+\theta_S\,b_t\,\text{dep}^{TW}_t\,x^{\text{eff}}_{\text{chips},t}-\theta_D\tanh\tfrac{\text{lead}_t}{\tau}-\theta_I(I_t-I_0)+\epsilon_t\Big). \]

The intercept \(\theta_0\) is solved so that cumulative incidence \(1-\prod_t(1-h_t)\) by 2040 under Maintain matches the stated baseline. A cumulative target is never applied as an annual increment. A war can deny the US Taiwanese fabs, capture them, or stay limited, and each costs differently. The catastrophe hazard peaks around the transition. A long lead for whoever is ahead lowers it, because it buys time to test systems, and a close race under tension raises it. The hazard is summed over the two developers, each weighted by its own proximity to the threshold, so a trailing developer adds risk while it closes in. Chinese development carries a nationality multiplier, set explicitly in each worldview (1 = no difference). The leader's reduction from its lead is scaled by the share of slack actually spent on safety, which is low if labs in the leading country race each other. Because a catastrophe has no feedback on the simulated state, its loss is valued as an exact expectation along each path, \(1-\prod_t(1-h^{\text{cat}}_t)\), rather than from rare simulated events; the outcome probabilities still report simulated events.

Payoffs: a vector, aggregated last

Each world yields discounted components:

  • AI-driven output for the US, China and the rest of the world, including idea spillovers between them;
  • trade: export profit net of sales that would have happened anyway, retaliation, decoupling and subsidy costs;
  • openness gains and the markups paid to closed providers;
  • the leader's capture of others' contestable post-transition surplus, and the cost to every economy if a durable order turns out institutionally adverse;
  • war and catastrophe losses;
  • indices of exposure to coercion, liberty and integration.

There is no "winner" jackpot. The value of a lead comes only from the capture and coercion that the lead, capture share and persistence actually produce. A US firm's lost Chinese revenue is not a dollar-for-dollar US loss: when supply is scarce, a sale to China displaces a sale elsewhere. The three value sets weight the same vector differently, so empirical and ethical disagreements stay separable.

What this model does and does not claim

There are three kinds of claim here. A scenario runs under China's assumed response rule. A policy comparison holds inside one causal model. An equilibrium is one of a specified coarse game, reported with deviation gains. None of these is the true geopolitical equilibrium. The parameters are mostly illustrative. The calibration anchors (the 2026 compute ratio, the capability lag, domestic market shares, GDP, war costs) are sourced but loosely mapped. Emergent quantities, such as the extra domestic adoption that controls cause or the long-run compute ratio, are outputs to check against intuition and evidence, not inputs.

Reproduce

The Python package runs large Monte Carlo comparisons, break-even grids, global sensitivity with value of information, stress tests and the strategic game:

pip install -e ".[dev]"
python -m ai_controls.cli compare --configs configs/fox_continuous.yaml \
    configs/erich_decisive.yaml configs/agnostic_wide.yaml --runs 10000 --seed 42
python -m ai_controls.cli sensitivity --config configs/agnostic_wide.yaml --runs 8000
pytest
