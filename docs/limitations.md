# Limitations

- **Mostly illustrative parameters.** The structure is meant to be right about which mechanisms exist and how they interact. The magnitudes are not measured. Treat any single number as conditional on the assumptions table.
- **The Chinese response is an assumed rule.** The strategic-game section compares four stance multipliers under China's own beliefs and objective. That is a coarse grid, not an optimising agent with a horizon, and best-response iteration does not establish that an equilibrium exists, is unique or converges.
- **Reduced-form industry.** Logic, HBM and packaging capacity, yields and know-how are indices with placeholder learning curves. Firm-level choices, process nodes, specific tool classes, servicing restrictions and third-country export controls (Netherlands, Japan, Korea) are not modelled separately.
- **Reduced-form power.** Datacentre power follows a build rule: keep the 2026 spare capacity, no faster than a maximum rate. Chip deliveries do not respond to power, so while US power binds, chips are delivered and wait. Grid politics, siting abroad beyond what the growth rate allows, electricity prices and the operating cost of inefficient chips are not modelled.
- **Reduced-form capability.** Capability is log training compute plus an algorithmic index. Data, talent, deployment feedback and distillation enter only through diffusion and the follower floor. Training and inference are split by a fixed share.
- **Thresholds are labels on continuous processes.** "Arrival", "durable" and "near-simultaneous" are cut-offs on continuous quantities, chosen for reporting. The payoffs use the continuous quantities, not the labels.
- **Conflict.** The war hazard is a logit with four channels and calibrated shocks. Alliance politics, nuclear dynamics, domestic regime security and crisis bargaining are not modelled. War types and their costs are coarse.
- **Catastrophe.** The hazard is a stylised function of proximity, lead and race intensity. Its size and severity are normative and scenario choices as much as empirical ones.
- **Payoffs.** The GDP uplift from AI, post-transition growth, contestable shares and the cost of adverse institutions are illustrative. Liberty, security and integration are indices valued by the weight sets, and their dollar weights are value judgments.
- **One reference starting state.** The model evaluates policies from the 2026 state. A counterfactual with no controls since 2022 is a separate question that this model does not answer.
- **Monte Carlo noise.** Paired draws, and exact expected catastrophe losses, keep comparisons precise. War events still add noise, so use the reported standard errors and more worlds for close calls. The site runs a few hundred worlds per policy, and the Python package is meant for large runs.
- **Global sensitivity.** The given-data first-order indices are small, because most of the variance in a policy difference comes from world-level shocks (war timing, decisiveness draws) rather than from any single parameter. The expected value of perfect information is the more useful ranking for deciding what to research next.
